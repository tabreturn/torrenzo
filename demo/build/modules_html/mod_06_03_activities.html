Resources

Principles of Food Preservation (Ahmed & Kowalski, 2021)

Chapter 7, 'Fruit Preservation: Chemistry and Practice' (pp. 203–245), covers the fundamental chemistry of food spoilage and the mechanisms by which drying, freezing, sugar preservation, and canning prevent it. The chapter includes practical protocols for home-scale preservation experiments.

Access Principles of Food Preservation

The Chemistry of Fruit Browning (Santos, 2022)

Enzymatic browning – the process that turns cut apples and avocados brown – is one of the most visible signs of fruit degradation. This paper explains the chemistry behind browning, the factors that influence its rate, and how different preservation methods slow or prevent it.

Access The Chemistry of Fruit Browning

Documenting Change: Before-and-After Photography in Food Science (Kowalski, 2023)

This practical guide covers techniques for documenting food transformations photographically. It addresses lighting consistency, colour calibration, scale reference, and framing – all essential for producing reliable before-and-after comparisons.

Access Documenting Change

Practice Activity

Conduct a preservation experiment with one fruit:

  1. Select a fruit that is ripe but firm. Photograph it from standardised angles with a scale reference (a ruler or coin).
  2. Divide the fruit into three equal portions and apply three preservation methods: drying, freezing, and sugar maceration.
  3. After 24 hours, photograph each preserved sample from the same angles with the same lighting and scale reference.
  4. Write a 300-word reflection comparing the three outcomes. Which method best preserved the fruit's original character? Which created the most interesting transformation? What does each outcome tell you about the fruit's structure and composition?
  5. Share your before-and-after photographs and reflection in the facilitated session.
