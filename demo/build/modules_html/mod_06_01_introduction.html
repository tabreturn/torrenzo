Module 6.1: Introduction to Fruit Preservation

Introduction

Fruit is ephemeral. A perfectly ripe mango has a window of perhaps 48 hours before it passes into overripeness and decay. Throughout human history, people have developed techniques to extend this window: drying, sugaring, pickling, fermenting, freezing, and canning. Each method transforms the fruit, sometimes subtly and sometimes dramatically, creating new textures, concentrated flavours, and entirely new food products.

Understanding preservation is essential for fruit documentation because it reveals how a fruit's structure and composition respond to environmental stress. A fruit that browns quickly when cut has high polyphenol oxidase activity. A fruit that collapses into mush when frozen has fragile cell walls. These responses tell you something fundamental about the fruit's biology.[1]

Assessment Progression

Assessment 2 requires you to apply a preservation method and document the before-and-after characteristics of your chosen fruits. This module provides the theoretical background and practical techniques you need.

The preservation reflection component of Assessment 2 should not simply describe what happened – it should demonstrate your understanding of why the fruit responded as it did. Use the concepts from this module to explain your observations.
