Module 5.1: Introduction to Exotic Tropical Fruits

Introduction

Beyond the familiar oranges, mangoes, and berries lies a world of fruits that challenge our expectations of what fruit can look, smell, and taste like. Dragon fruit with its shocking pink skin and speckled white flesh, rambutan with its hairy red exterior hiding translucent sweetness, and durian – famously banned from hotels and public transport across Southeast Asia – with its formidable aroma and custard-like flesh.

These exotic fruits are excellent subjects for sensory observation precisely because they are unfamiliar. Without preconceptions, you must rely entirely on what your senses tell you. This makes the observation process more deliberate and often more revealing.[1]

Subject Learning Outcomes

This module will help you achieve the following outcomes:

  • (a) Identify and describe tropical fruits using sensory vocabulary like aroma, sweetness, and texture.
  • (d) Share a brief write-up that compares two different fruits and what makes each one unique in presentation and flavor.
  • (g) Curate a digital collection with consistent metadata, tagging, and visual presentation standards.

Assessment Progression

For Assessment 3, your digital exhibition must include at least three fruits from outside the citrus and stone fruit families. The exotic fruits in this module provide excellent material for creating visually striking and sensorially distinctive entries.

As you explore these fruits, think about how you would present them in a curated gallery. What story does each fruit tell? How would you sequence them to create a compelling narrative journey?
