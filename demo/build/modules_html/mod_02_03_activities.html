Resources

The Stone Fruit Family (Patel & Okafor, 2023)

Chapter 5, 'Drupes Across Climates' (pp. 112–138), surveys the global diversity of stone fruits, from temperate cherries and peaches to tropical mangoes and lychees. The chapter explains how the drupe structure – a fleshy mesocarp surrounding a hardened endocarp – has evolved independently in multiple plant families and how this structure influences fruit handling, storage, and consumption.

Access The Stone Fruit Family

Mango Cultivars of the World (Thongkham, 2021)

This comprehensive guide profiles over 50 mango cultivars, comparing their flavour profiles, fibre content, ripening behaviour, and commercial availability. The sensory evaluation section provides a useful vocabulary for describing mango flavour notes and a structured tasting protocol that you can adapt for your own observations.

Access Mango Cultivars of the World

Comparing Fruits Systematically (O'Brien, 2022)

This methodology article introduces structured comparison frameworks for food products. The author presents a matrix approach that organises observations across sensory, structural, and compositional dimensions. The framework is designed to reduce subjective bias and produce comparison data that can be shared and replicated.

Access Comparing Fruits Systematically

Peaches and Nectarines in Subtropical Regions (Gupta, 2020)

This research paper examines how low-chill peach and nectarine varieties have expanded stone fruit production into subtropical and tropical regions. The paper discusses how growing conditions affect fruit characteristics, including skin colour development, sugar accumulation, and texture.

Access Peaches and Nectarines in Subtropical Regions

Practice Activity

Choose one stone fruit and complete a structured observation. Record your observations immediately after cutting the fruit, and then again after 30 minutes. Note any changes in aroma or appearance caused by oxidation.
