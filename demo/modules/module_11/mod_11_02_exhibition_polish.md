# Module 11.2: Polishing Your Exhibition

## From Draft to Exhibition

*A polished exhibition feels effortless to browse. But that effortlessness is the result of careful editing, consistent design, and attention to detail.*

### Reviewing Your Entries

Before you finalise your exhibition, review each entry against these criteria:

**Photography**
- [ ] All photographs are sharp, well-lit, and colour-accurate
- [ ] Background, lighting, and composition are consistent across entries
- [ ] Each fruit has at least one photograph showing it whole and one showing internal structure
- [ ] All image attributions are correct and complete

**Descriptions**
- [ ] Each entry includes a 100--150 word sensory description
- [ ] Descriptions use varied sensory vocabulary (not just "sweet" and "tasty")
- [ ] Each description helps the reader imagine what experiencing the fruit would be like
- [ ] Tone is consistent across entries

**Cultural and Botanical Notes**
- [ ] Each entry includes relevant cultural or botanical context
- [ ] Information is accurate and appropriately referenced
- [ ] Notes add depth without overwhelming the sensory description
- [ ] Connections between entries (shared origin, related species, contrasting uses) are noted

**Metadata**
- [ ] All tags are applied consistently using your controlled vocabulary
- [ ] Tag categories are meaningful and useful for browsing
- [ ] The tag index or filter system works correctly
- [ ] Navigation between entries is intuitive

### The Curator's Reflection

Your 500-word curator's reflection is your opportunity to explain the thinking behind your exhibition. A strong reflection:

1. **Explains your selection**: Why these ten fruits? What criteria did you use?
2. **Describes your curatorial approach**: How did you structure the exhibition? What narrative or theme guided your sequencing?
3. **Reflects on collaboration**: How did you and your partner divide the work? What did each of you contribute?
4. **Identifies challenges**: What was difficult? How did you overcome problems?
5. **Articulates learning**: What did you learn about curation, collaboration, and communication through this project?
6. **Considers alternatives**: If you were to do it again, what would you do differently?

The reflection should demonstrate critical self-awareness, not just describe what you did. The best reflections show that you understand why you made the choices you made and can evaluate those choices honestly.[@marshall2020catalogue]

### The README

Your README.md file is a practical document that ensures your submission can be understood and assessed. It should include:

```
# Digital Fruit Exhibition: [Your Exhibition Title]

## Creators
- [Student Name], [Student ID]
- [Student Name], [Student ID]

## Contents
- exhibition.html (or .md) -- Main exhibition file
- reflection.md -- Curator's reflection
- assets/ -- Image files
  - fruit01.jpg -- [description and source]
  - fruit02.jpg -- [description and source]
  - ... (all images listed)

## Technical Notes
[Any information the assessor needs to view the exhibition correctly,
e.g., recommended browser, required fonts, display settings]

## Image Attributions
- [image filename]: [photographer/creator], [license/source], [date]
- ... (all images attributed)
```

### Final Checks

Before submission, test your exhibition:

1. Open it on a different computer or device from the one you built it on.
2. Check that all images load correctly.
3. Verify that all links, tags, and navigation elements work.
4. Read every entry aloud -- does it sound natural? Are there typos?
5. Have a friend browse the exhibition and tell you what they learned.

Fix any issues before the submission deadline.

## Practice Activity

Exchange exhibitions with another pair. Using the Assessment 3 rubric, provide constructive feedback on:
- Fruit selection and diversity
- Quality of sensory descriptions
- Metadata and organisation
- Visual presentation
- Overall curatorial cohesion

Use the feedback to make final improvements to your own exhibition before submission.
