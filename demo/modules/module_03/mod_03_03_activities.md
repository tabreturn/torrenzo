# Module 3.3: Activities

## Resources

<section>

### The Science of Small Fruits (Johansson & Lee, 2021)

This chapter explores the botany and chemistry of berries, including the structural differences between true berries, aggregate fruits, and multiple fruits. The section on anthocyanin chemistry explains why berry colour is both visually striking and functionally important for seed dispersal.

Access [The Science of Small Fruits](https://orangeuni.edu/library)

</section>

<section>

### Photographing Small Subjects (Chen & Ibrahim, 2022)

While this reading also appears in Module 1, the techniques it describes are especially relevant to berries. Small fruits require careful attention to lighting, depth of field, and background selection. The close-up photography techniques in this chapter will help you capture the fine details of berry surfaces and internal structures.

Access [Photographing Food and Natural Objects](https://orangeuni.edu/library)

</section>

<section>

### Sensory Evaluation of Berries (Mwangi, 2023)

This study presents a standardised protocol for berry sensory evaluation, including scales for sweetness, acidity, aroma intensity, and texture. The protocol is designed to be reproducible across different evaluators and fruit samples, making it useful for comparative analysis.

Access [Sensory Evaluation of Berries](https://orangeuni.edu/library)

</section>

## Practice Activity

Visit a market or grocery store and purchase three different berries. For each, record:

1. A photograph of the whole fruit, a cut cross-section, and a close-up of the surface
2. A 50-word sensory description covering appearance, aroma, texture, and flavour
3. A subjective rating (1--5) on each of: sweetness, acidity, aroma intensity, and visual appeal

Arrange your observations in a simple table. Notice which dimensions show the most variation across your three samples.
