# Module 5.3: Activities

## Resources

<section>

### Exotic Fruits of Southeast Asia (Tan & Widiastuti, 2021)

This beautifully illustrated guide profiles over 40 tropical fruits from the markets, gardens, and forests of Southeast Asia. Each entry includes photographs, flavour descriptions, cultural notes, and guidance on how to select and prepare the fruit. An excellent resource for Assessment 3 research.

Access [Exotic Fruits of Southeast Asia](https://orangeuni.edu/library)

</section>

<section>

### The Durian: Botany, Culture, and Controversy (Lim, 2020)

This monograph is the definitive English-language work on durian. It covers the fruit's botany, its historical role in Southeast Asian culture and trade, the chemistry of its volatile aroma compounds, and its contested place in modern urban life. Even if you cannot taste a durian yourself, this book will give you a rich understanding of why this fruit inspires such passionate responses.

Access [The Durian: Botany, Culture, and Controversy](https://orangeuni.edu/library)

</section>

<section>

### Visual Culture and Tropical Fruit (Fernandez, 2022)

This article examines how tropical fruits have been represented in art, advertising, and popular media from the colonial era to the present. The author argues that the visual language used to depict tropical fruits reflects and shapes Western perceptions of the tropics, often emphasising exoticism and abundance while erasing agricultural and cultural contexts.

Access [Visual Culture and Tropical Fruit](https://orangeuni.edu/library)

</section>

## Practice Activity

Research three exotic fruits you have never tasted. For each, compile:

1. Three high-quality photographs (whole fruit, cut open, and in context -- on a tree, in a market, or in a dish)
2. A 100-word sensory profile synthesised from published descriptions and tasting notes
3. Cultural context: origin, traditional uses, symbolism, and any interesting stories or taboos
4. A reflection on what sensory information is missing from your virtual observation and how you might fill those gaps if you could access the fresh fruit

Present your research as a mini-exhibition -- a single page with images and descriptions arranged to tell a story about the three fruits you chose.
