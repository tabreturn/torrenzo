# Module 2.2: The Mango

## The World's Most Popular Fruit

*Over 100 million tonnes of mangoes are produced globally each year, making them one of the most consumed fruits on the planet.*

Mangoes (*Mangifera indica*) have been cultivated in South Asia for over 4,000 years and are now grown throughout the tropics and subtropics. The mango's appeal lies in its extraordinary flavour complexity: a single fruit can carry notes of peach, pineapple, coconut, and citrus, all layered over a distinctive resinous sweetness that varies dramatically between cultivars.

When observing a mango, start with the skin. Ripe mangoes display a gradient of colour -- from green through yellow to deep orange and red -- but colour is not a reliable indicator of ripeness. The skin is smooth but may have small lenticels (pores) visible on the surface. Press gently: a ripe mango yields slightly, like a ripe avocado.@harper2021sensory

Cutting into a mango reveals its most distinctive feature: the large, flat pit that clings tightly to the flesh. The flesh itself ranges from deep golden-yellow to pale cream, and its texture varies from completely fibre-free in varieties like Nam Doc Mai to distinctly fibrous in varieties like Tommy Atkins. The aroma intensifies as you approach the pit, where volatile compounds are most concentrated.

Mangoes are classified by their embryo type: monoembryonic varieties (most Indian cultivars) produce fruit that varies from the parent tree, while polyembryonic varieties (most Southeast Asian cultivars) produce fruit true to type. This botanical distinction has shaped centuries of mango cultivation and trade, influencing which varieties became globally dominant.@ramirez2021citrus

In terms of flavour, a good mango balances sweetness with a subtle acidic undertone. The sugar content can exceed 20% in fully ripe fruit, but the perception of sweetness is modulated by the presence of aromatic compounds that give each variety its distinctive character. The Alphonso mango, for example, is prized for its intense saffron-and-honey notes, while the Kent mango offers a milder, more accessible sweetness.

## Observation Exercise

Select two different mango varieties if available, or a mango and another stone fruit. Compare:

- Skin colour, texture, and thickness
- Flesh colour, fibre content, and juiciness
- Pit size relative to fruit size
- Aroma intensity at different distances from the pit
- Sweetness, acidity, and any distinctive flavour notes
