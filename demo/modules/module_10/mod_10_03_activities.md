# Module 10.3: Activities

## Resources

<section>

### Comparative Methods in Sensory Science (O'Brien, 2022)

This is the methodology article introduced in Module 2. Re-read it now with the experience of having completed multiple fruit comparisons. Pay attention to the sections on avoiding bias and ensuring reproducibility in comparative analysis.

Access [Comparing Fruits Systematically](https://orangeuni.edu/library)

</section>

<section>

### Writing Comparative Analyses (Hoffman, 2021)

This writing guide focuses specifically on comparative analysis in scientific and professional contexts. It covers structuring comparisons, using comparative language precisely, and presenting comparison data in tables and figures.

Access [Writing Comparative Analyses](https://orangeuni.edu/library)

</section>

<section>

### Statistical Thinking for Sensory Data (Rahman, 2022)

While your assessment does not require statistical analysis, understanding basic concepts like variability, central tendency, and correlation will strengthen your comparative thinking. This accessible introduction assumes no prior statistical knowledge.

Access [Statistical Thinking for Sensory Data](https://orangeuni.edu/library)

</section>

## Practice Activity

This activity is designed to be completed with your Assessment 2 fruits. If you have not selected your fruits yet, use any two fruits for practice.

1. Complete the full comparison framework from Module 10.2 (sensory matrix, structural comparison, transformation comparison).
2. Draft your comparison section (300 words).
3. Review your draft against the Assessment 2 rubric. Does your comparison:
   - Cover at least six dimensions?
   - Use specific, sensory language?
   - Draw meaningful conclusions rather than just listing differences?
   - Connect observations to underlying structure or composition?
4. Exchange drafts with a peer and provide feedback on each other's comparisons.

Refine your draft based on feedback and prepare to integrate it into your full Assessment 2 report.
